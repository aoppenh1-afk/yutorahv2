# Stripes on a טלית

## הקדמה

- **Q:** where do stripes on a טלית come from, is it a מנהג ישראל or just something we do
    - Many people have black stripes, some have blue, some have white, and some have all colors
    - The source is three possibilities mentioned by the ספרים

## Three possible sources for stripes

- **פרי מגדים (סימן ט׳)**
    - Stripes are a זכר לתכלת because for many years Jewish people didn't have תכלת
        - **Q:** now that I have תכלת, should I not have stripes on my טלית
        - **A:** still allowed to have stripes
        - Only a wool טלית קטן has black stripes, but a cotton טלית קטן doesn't
            - Because תכלת only works on wool since by definition it is wool, so a זכר to תכלת has to be on a wool בגד
- **זוהר**
    - Based on קבלה, black represents the מדה of גבורה and white represents the מדה of חסד or רחמים
        - The fusion of גבורה and רחמים should be represented in the טלית, so it is not only white, which is the primary color, but also black or blue, which is the secondary color
- **משנה (מסכת ברכות)**
    - The משנה discusses the זמנים in the morning and says the earliest זמן to say קריאת שמע is when a person can be מבחין בין תכלת ללבן
        - If you have it right in front of you and can see that you have a white בגד with a תכלת stripe on it, then you can tell whether you are able to be מבחין בין תכלת ללבן
        - So stripes may have been included on the טלית not as the מצוה דאורייתא of תכלת, but to function for one of the things תכלת helped with, telling the זמן for קריאת שמע

## Why black, blue, or white stripes

- Black is the most common nowadays
    - **רמב״ם (הלכות ציצית פרק ב׳)**
        - The דם החלזון they used was a black דם, although once it went through the process it actually produced a blue dye and not a black dye
    - **גמ׳ מנחות**
        - תכלת is דומה לים and ים is דומה לרקיע, so תכלת is somehow the color of the ים or the רקיע, but the גמ׳ doesn't say explicitly at what time of day
            - **רש״י:** it is the רקיע toward evening time, which would be a much darker blue
            - **רמב״ם:** it is the middle of the day
        - So if תכלת is supposed to be black, the stripes should be black, or even if it is a blackish color, a very dark blue that almost appears black
    - The תורה itself is referred to as אש שחורה על גבי אש לבנה
        - White is purity and innocence, and the black lines or letters of the תורה are the אש שחורה on top of the אש לבנה, and the טלית is representative of that
        - **רש״י בחומש** and the **גמ׳** say ציצית remind us of all תרי״ג מצות, so the בגד on which you put the ציצית should somehow represent the entirety of תורה
- Blue used to be the more common מנהג but grew out of favor in more contemporary times
    - **David Wolffsohn**
        - When the design proposed by Theodor Herzl for the Israeli flag wasn't gaining support, he wrote "At the behest of our leader Herzl, I came to Basel to make preparations for the Zionist Congress. Among many other problems that occupied me then was one that contained something of the essence of the Jewish problem: What flag would we hang in the Congress Hall? Then an idea struck me: We have a flag, and it's blue and white! The טלית, with which we wrap ourselves when we pray, that is our symbol. So all I have to do is take the טלית out of the bag, slap a מגן דוד on it, and that's our flag"
        - That concept became the Israeli flag, and the assumption was that the טלית had blue stripes on it
- White stripes are certainly seen on יום כיפור because people always wear white as a symbol of purity, that אם יהיו חטאיכם כשנים כשלג ילבינו, even though we may have sins we hope they become whitened and purified
    - But even not on יום כיפור, there is a fairly common ספרדי practice to have white stripes
        - **ברכי יוסף (שיורי ברכה) in the name of the מהרש״ל**
            - **יש אומרים** that the טלית should be white
            - Could be partially based on what the **שולחן ערוך (סימן ט׳ סעיף ה׳)** writes, "יש אומרים שצריך לעשות הציצית מצבע הטלית, והמדקדקים נוהגים כן", and the **רמ״א** writes "והאשכנזים לא נהגו לעשות הציצית רק לבנים, אף בבגדים צבועים, ואין לשנות"
                - Put together that the ציצית are supposed to match the בגד, and our מנהג acc to the רמ״א is to use only white ציצית, so the בגד should be white
        - Also על פי קבלה, many ספרדים have דווקא fully white
            - **בן איש חי (פרשת נח)**
                - It doesn't bother him that there are stripes of a different color: "יזהר לעשות הטליות והחוטים של הציציות הכל לבן, בין בטלית גדול בין בטלית קטן, רמז לדבר עטוף בלבן. וטליתות שיש בהן צבע מצדדים לנוי, לא אכפת"
                - Because "כי הצבע הוא מעט מן המעט בגוף הטלית דרובו לבן", it is mostly white, so that fulfills the דין
        - Even though white is the more common ספרדי practice, you find pictures of famous ספרדי rabbis with black stripes
            - **Ex:** רב בנציון אבא שאול with black stripes, רב כדורי with black stripes, and רב עובדיה mostly white but in earlier years also with black stripes

## Summary

- Stripes are (I) זכר לתכלת, (II) על פי קבלה as a fusion of גבורה and חסד, or (III) to be מבחין בין תכלת ללבן for קריאת שמע, and the color is black acc to a dark understanding of תכלת or אש שחורה על גבי אש לבנה, blue acc to the older מנהג preserved in the Israeli flag, or white acc to matching white ציצית and עטוף בלבן
