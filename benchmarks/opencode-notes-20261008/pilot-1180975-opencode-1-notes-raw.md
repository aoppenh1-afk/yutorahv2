# Giving Two Names

## הקדמה

- **Case:** request to discuss giving a child two names because in תנ״ך and גמ׳ and ראשונים almost nobody has two names, everybody seems to have one name
- **Q:** why did the מנהג start to give more than one name
    - Was there a mass casualty event where many people needed to be named after and not enough children to bear all the names, so they had to double up
    - Was it one community that started it because of שלום בית, where one side wanted to name after their parent and the other side after their parent, so they compromised with both names
    - Was it because there were Yiddish translations of names, so once you had Hebrew and Yiddish like יששכר בער and צבי הירש, then they came up with other double and triple names
    - Not going to get into that
- What shiur will discuss
    - Do we ever find it existed in earlier times, in גמ׳, in ראשונים
    - How do we interpret it when it seems like in גמ׳ people had two names
    - Is there anything wrong with it, when you cannot decide who to name after, or when you add on another name because you do not want to saddle the child with bubbe's name like Freidel for her entire life
    - Is that okay to add another name or to name after two people

## שיטת נודע ביהודה

- **נודע ביהודה מהדורא תנינא אורח חיים סימן קי״ג**
    - With weakness of memory, does not think there is a single case in all of ש״ס, בבלי, ירושלמי, where a תנא or אמורא has more than one name, has two names
- **Q:** what do we do with cases in ש״ס that seem to be two names
    - **A:** in ש״ס it was not at all prevalent, and almost always one of those names is not his real name, it is just a title
- **רב אושעיא בריבי, רבי אלעזר הקפר בריבי, חזקיה בריבי**
    - When בריבי is mentioned alone, that is a name, not a title
    - Whenever בריבי is added on to somebody else's name, רש״י consistently says בריבי is also a title, that he was the גדול הדור, the biggest person in the generation
        - Have this in מסכת חולין in a couple of places
    - When בריבי appears alone, רש״י will always say it is שם חכם, because רש״י has to tell you that, because usually it is not שם חכם, usually it is a title added on to somebody else's name
    - **Q:** one exception in חולין דף י״א, and two places in ש״ס where בריבי is used alone and רש״י says it refers to other well-known אמוראים, חזקיה and רבי יוסי
        - **A:** in both places, חולין דף נ״ז and סוטה דף כ״ט, it is clear from context who it is referring to, so רש״י is not telling a big חידוש that it refers to some other אמורא, so that is not a double name
- **אבא שאול, אבא יוסי**
    - Term אבא is also a title that indicates importance of a person
    - Just like you refer to your father as אבא as a way of showing כבוד, so when they referred to someone of great authority they called him אבא
    - That is why you never find an עבד who has the title אבא, except for the עבדים של רבן גמליאל, because they were so חשוב that they had the title of אבא
- **אימא שלום, wife of רבי אלעזר**
    - Not because she had two names, her name was שלום, she was called אימא שלום because she was such a חשובה lady that she got the name אימא
    - Parenthetically, somewhat interesting that שלום was a woman's name, maybe a unisex name like יונה is used now for boys and girls even though in תנ״ך it is used for boys and birds
- **עי מרי**
    - Someone referred to in גמ׳ by that name, appears in גיטין on דף ל״ה
        - **רש״י:** it is the person's שם חניכה, some sort of surname or nickname
        - **ספר תורת חסד:** you could argue that for a שם חניכה people would use two names, but not for a regular name
        - It could also be that עי is a title much like the name אבא, that it is also a title

## שיטת ראשונים

- In times of ראשונים it is exceedingly rare to find two names, but it is not unprecedented, it was not unheard of
- **יעקב ישראל, a בעל התוספות mentioned very rarely**
    - Appears in תוס׳ in כתובות on דף צ״ח, and in חולין on דף קי״ב, and in a number of other ראשונים outside of תוס׳, in ספר הישר לרבינו תם
    - His name was יעקב ישראל, who is יעקב and ישראל were both the same person, you get the impression that is where it started
- **תנ״ך**
    - משה רבינו had a lot of names, יתרו had a lot of names, but they are never referred to by more than one of those names at the same time, so that does not count in our discussion
- **מהרש״ל in ים של שלמה on גיטין in פרק ד׳, brought as ראיה by חתם סופר that מנהג used to be to give only one name**
    - There was once a debate between a husband and wife who to name their son after, husband wanted מאיר after his father, wife wanted יאיר after her father
    - They said מאיר, יאיר both relate to אור, so they named the baby שניאור, שני אור, naming after two sources of light, and that is how the name שניאור came to be
    - **Q:** why not just call him יאיר מאיר, that would have solved the problem
        - **A:** אלא מאי, ים של שלמה seems to assume that was unheard of, who would name a baby two names, so they had to pick one name that encapsulated both names
        - **A1:** one could argue naming him מאיר יאיר would not have solved the problem, because then the other one would have said why isn't he יאיר מאיר, why do you get the first name, I should get the first name, so this was the only way to solve the problem
    - Ample evidence that the word שניאור existed before that time
        - **רמב״ן:** occasionally mentions the name שניאור, way before the time of ים של שלמה
        - **חיד״א in מערכת גדולים:** רבינו יונה refers to a רבי named שניאור as well, one of his רביים
        - Could be the name existed and they chose that name because they felt it encapsulated both of their fathers
- Where did מנהג start, we do not know
    - Do you find it in תנ״ך, pretty much never
    - Do you find it in גמ׳, pretty much never
    - Do you find it in ראשונים, you do find it, but very, very, very rarely

## Is There Anything Wrong With Two Names

- It is relatively recent vintage
- **Distinction:** giving two names in general, like after the same person or just picking two names you like, versus giving two names after two different people
    - Another element is adding a second name due to illness, which is another way people have an extra name
- **Adding a name due to illness**
    - **גמ׳ ראש השנה דף ט״ז and תענית דף ט״ז and בבא קמא דף קכ״ה:** one of the ways of removing a גזירה רעה against a person is to change a person's name
    - **בית יוסף in יורה דעה סימן של״ח:** it was fairly common practice to change someone's name in the face of terrible illness in hopes to remove the terrible decree
    - **גמ׳ recommends:** שינוי השם will be משנה the person's מזל, so typically when a person is very desperately ill, will change the person's name
    - **Ex:** Rav Belsky זכרונו לברכה was Rav Yisroel Belsky, and when he died he was Rav Chaim Yisroel Belsky, because about seven years before he died he was very sick and they added the name חיים to him
        - It sticks if he gets better, if he does not get better and dies from the illness, then you remove that name and it does not stick
        - Many people wake up from a coma to discover their name changed, and they live the rest of life with a new name, obviously the better of the two outcomes
    - Usually with consultation of גדולים, because we try not to do it on our own
        - מדרש says when parents name a child there is a sense of רוח הקודש, that is when the baby is young, who has that רוח הקודש later on
        - Only people with that level are שוטים, deranged people, the גמ׳ says, or you need a big צדיק who you trust has that sense of רוח הקודש
- **נודע ביהודה and חתם סופר**
    - Do not explicitly אוסר it, but their tone suggests some sort of negative feeling about it
- **חזון איש, reported by Rav Sternbuch in תשובות והנהגות חלק א׳ סימן תר״ו**
    - Did not like the מנהג at all of giving two names, does not really give a reason, but presumably because of complexities in הלכות גיטין
        - What is a real name, what is not a real name, what do you call him if you only call him by one of the names and not both, דמיקרי, becomes very complex when writing גיטין
        - People get nervous writing כתובות, lower-stakes issue, when writing a גט dealing with יחוס of כלל ישראל, have to be super-duper careful getting every possible name right
    - Of course חזון איש's name was אברהם ישעיה, he had two names, but he did not name himself, his parents named him
- **אגרות משה אורח חיים חלק ה׳ סימן י׳ אות ג׳**
    - הקריאה בשני שמות יחד לא היה זה בדורות הקדמונים, it did not exist, אף אחר זמן הגאונים, even after the time of גאונים
    - Quotes מהרש״ל in גיטין, אין דרך להיות לאדם שני שמות כאחד
    - Quotes נחלת שבעה, ואם ניתן לו שני שמות [ברית: יוסף ויצחק] ושניהם כאחד כאשר שמעתי שבמקצת מקומות נהגו כן, says I heard there are some places that at the ברית they give the baby two names, implication he heard it happened somewhere else but wherever he was they did not do that
    - Says this is uncommon
    - אבל אינו נוגע לנו לידע סיבת הדבר, not so important to know why this happened
    - דאף שהיה זה שלא כהוגן ומיחו בהם, historically it was not right and probably the rabbis got angry and told them they should not do this
    - מכיון שלא היה על זה איסור גמור לא הועיל כידוע, since it is not an איסור גמור, rabbis yelling and screaming does not help
        - When rabbis yell about something that is an איסור גמור, כלל ישראל feels it and eventually obeys, when it is not an איסור גמור, many times that does not help
    - אך טעם זה מועיל לסלק אף הגינאי שלא יהא על ישראל, אף לא על סתמן שאינם בני תורה, שמכל מקום נאיסו על דרך כו׳, so essentially says there is nothing wrong with it בזמן הזה
    - חזון איש was somewhat of a דעת יחיד on this issue

## Naming After Two Different People

- Sometimes easiest way to resolve שלום בית issue, so assuming no objection to give a child two names
- **ספר ברית אבות quotes from a number of גדולים who say not to name a child after two different people**
    - Particularly two people who did not get along with one another in their lifetimes, which unfortunately is sometimes the case when it is mother's father and father's father, מחותנים who often had their own issues with one another
    - Presumably based on kabbalistic considerations
- **Q:** combination of two names may be considered not really naming after anybody, a third independent name, so whole point of extending that מזל and זכות for that נשמה is lost
    - Rav Schechter quoted from חזון איש that when you name, depending which way it is a זכות for that family member, when you name after a family member it is a זכות for that family member, when you name after a צדיק it is a זכות for the baby, but it could be a זכות for nobody if it is not the same name at all
    - Meaning if one זיידע was Avraham and other זיידע was Yitzchak, and you name baby Avraham Yitzchak, could be he is named after neither זיידע
- **Rav Leizer Silver, printed in משולחן גבוה at end of פרשת מטות**
    - Said somebody named Yitzchak Isaac was allowed to name his child Avraham Yitzchak, because Avraham Yitzchak and Yitzchak Isaac have nothing to do with each other, Yitzchak Isaac is Yitzchak Isaac, Avraham Yitzchak is a different name
        - Like boxer George Foreman had George II, George III, George IV, George V
    - This was a פסק for Telz ראשי ישיבה, Rav Yitzchok Isaac Ausband named his child Rav Avraham Yitzchok Ausband
    - Opened a חומש to end of פרשת מטות
        - תורה says יאיר son of מנשה went and captured חוות, captured villages, and those villages were renamed חוות יאיר
        - Next פסוק says נבח went and captured קנת and called it נבח בשמו
        - **Q:** why does he have to say נבח בשמו
        - **A:** if you capture something and name a village יאיר after your name, if you name it straight יאיר, that would be בשמו, but he called it חוות יאיר, he added the word חוות, so it did not say בשמו, but נבח captured places and called it נבח straight, that is בשמו because he did not add anything, it was exactly same name
        - So you see that is called after his name, and once you change anything or add anything, then it is not the same name
- **דעת זקנים מבעלי התוספות**
    - יוסף named his son אפרים, says that was actually naming after his זיידע and עלטער זיידע, naming after אברהם אבינו and יצחק אבינו
    - Because אברהם refers to himself as אנכי עפר ואפר, and יצחק was like אפר על גבי המזבח with עקידה, so double אפר is אפרים
    - Implicit in that is apparently you can name for two different people even though it is not the exact name of either one, like story of מהרש״ל with מאיר and יאיר who became שניאור, where that is okay
    - תורה says why he was called אפרים, but אי נמי

## Non-Jewish Names

- **Q:** יוסף was called צפנת פענח, does that imply something about using non-Jewish names
    - **A:** that is a different topic about using non-Jewish names
- **Rav Moshe has discussion, somewhat controversial תשובה**
    - Brings many ראיות that many of the אמוראים had names that did not seem to be Jewish names
    - **Q:** how can we use non-Jewish names, thought one of reasons we were זוכה to גאולה was because שלא שינו את שמם, that we did not change our names
    - **A:** Rav Moshe has big חידוש, שלא שינו את שמם was because that is the only thing that made us Jewish, we did not have a תורה yet, so only thing that distinguished us was dress, language, names, once we have a תורה, תרי״ג מצוות distinguishes us, so שלא שינו את שמם is not a big deal
    - So you can name your child Bobby or Jeb or whatever you want
- **Q:** would there be issue with using Christian names, like John or Paul, historical figures
    - **A:** certainly any name that implies allusion to object of worship in other religions would be highly objectionable
    - Do people automatically think that, know a lot of Jews named Paul and John, hard to say those are אסור
    - **Ex:** Rabbeinu Peter, you do have that
    - Rav Moshe's תשובה about secular names brings lots of ראיות that people had that

## Summary

- (I) in גמ׳ pretty much never find two real names, בריבי, אבא, אימא, עי are titles or שם חניכה, (II) in ראשונים very rarely find it like יעקב ישראל, (III) acc to Rav Moshe historically שלא כהוגן but בזמן הזה nothing wrong with two names, (IV) naming after two different people some גדולים say not to, and combination may be a new name, though דעת זקנים holds אפרים covers עפר and אפר
