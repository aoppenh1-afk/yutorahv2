# קריאת שני שמות

## הקדמה

- Topic arose in connection with ברית מילה: Ari Katz asked about giving a child two names
    - Throughout תנ״ך, גמ׳, and ראשונים, almost everyone has only one name
- Questions the shiur will not address regarding why the מנהג to give multiple names began:
    - Mass casualty event requiring children to bear the names of multiple deceased relatives
    - שלום בית compromise between parents wishing to name after their respective parents
    - Combination of Hebrew and Yiddish names (e.g. יששכר בער, צבי הירש) which later expanded into double, triple, and quadruple names
- Questions the shiur will address:
    - Did giving two names exist historically in the times of the גמ׳ and ראשונים?
    - How do we interpret apparent instances in the גמ׳ where people have two names?
    - Is there anything halachically wrong with giving two names (e.g. due to indecision, avoiding an old-fashioned name like Bubbe Freidel, or naming after two people)?

## שני שמות בש״ס

- **נודע ביהודה (מהדורא תנינא, אורח חיים סימן קי״ג)**
    - States that despite weakness of memory, there is not a single case in all of ש״ס (בבלי or ירושלמי) where a תנא or an אמורא has two names
- **Q:** how do we explain apparent cases in ש״ס where people have two names?
    - **A (בריבי):**
        - Ex: רב אושעיא בריבי, רבי אלעזר הקפר בריבי, חזקיה בריבי
        - When בריבי appears alone, it is an actual name (שם חכם)
        - When בריבי is appended to another name, רש״י consistently explains it is a title indicating he was the גדול הדור (e.g. in מסכת חולין)
            - When it appears alone, רש״י explicitly notes it is a שם חכם precisely because בריבי is usually a title added to someone else's name
        - Exceptions noted by the נודע ביהודה:
            - One exception in חולין דף י״א
            - Two places where בריבי appears alone and רש״י explains it refers to other well-known אמוראים (חזקיה and רבי יוסי): חולין דף נ״ז and סוטה דף כ״ט
                - In both places the reference is clear from context, so it is not a double name
    - **A (אבא):**
        - Ex: אבא שאול, אבא יוסי
        - The term אבא is a title indicating importance and כבוד, modeled after calling one's father אבא
            - An עבד never receives the title אבא, except for the עבדים של רבן גמליאל because of their exceptional חשיבות
        - Similarly, רבי אלעזר's wife was called אימא שלום
            - Her actual name was שלום, and אימא was an honorific title due to her being an אשה חשובה
            - שלום was used as a woman's name (unisex, similar to how יונה is used today for both boys and girls)
    - **A (עי מרי):**
        - Mentioned in גמ׳ גיטין דף ל״ה
        - רש״י explains that this was a שם חניכה (surname or nickname)
        - **ספר תורת חסד**
            - Suggests people might use two names for a שם חניכה even if not for a standard given name
            - Alternatively, עי is an honorific title similar to אבא
- Conclusion: apparent double names in the גמ׳ are not two given names, but a name accompanied by a title or a שם חניכה

## תקופת הראשונים

- Having two names in the era of the ראשונים is exceedingly rare, but not unheard of
- **תוס׳**
    - Mentions a בעל התוספות named יעקב ישראל (תוס׳ כתובות דף צ״ח, חולין דף קי״ב, and ספר הישר לרבינו תם)
        - Both יעקב and ישראל were names of the same individual in תנ״ך, which may suggest how the practice began
- In תנ״ך, individuals like משה רבינו and יתרו possessed multiple names, but were never called by more than one name simultaneously
- **חתם סופר**
    - Proves the early מנהג was to give only one name from an account in the מהרש״ל
- **מהרש״ל (ים של שלמה גיטין פרק ד׳)**
    - Case: a husband and wife argued over naming their newborn son, the husband wanting to name after his father מאיר and the wife wanting to name after her father יאיר
    - They compromised by naming the child שניאור (meaning שני אור, two sources of light), encapsulating both fathers
    - **Q:** why not simply give the child both names and call him יאיר מאיר?
    - **A:** the ים של שלמה assumes giving two names was completely unheard of at that time
        - One could argue that calling him מאיר יאיר would not resolve the dispute, since the parents would still argue over whose father's name comes first
    - The name שניאור existed prior to that time:
        - רמב״ן mentions the name שניאור
        - **חיד״א (מערכת גדולים)**
            - Notes that רבינו יונה refers to a rebbe named שניאור
        - The parents chose an existing name because it conveniently represented both fathers

## Is There Anything Wrong with Giving Two Names?

- Giving multiple names is a relatively recent practice
- General tone of earlier פוסקים:
    - נודע ביהודה and חתם סופר do not explicitly forbid giving two names, but their tone conveys negative sentiment
- **חזון איש (תשובות והנהגות חלק א׳, סימן תר״ו)**
    - Rav Sternbuch reports the חזון איש strongly disliked the מנהג of giving two names
        - Even though his own name was אברהם ישעיה, that was chosen by his parents
    - Reason: potential halachic complications in גיטין
        - Determining the primary name vs secondary name if a person is only called by one name (דמיקרי)
        - High stakes of יחוס in writing a גט make any ambiguity in names critical, unlike writing a כתובה
- **אגרות משה (אורח חיים חלק ה׳, סימן י׳, אות ג׳)**
    - The חזון איש is somewhat of a דעת יחיד on this issue
    - Notes that calling by two names together did not exist in earlier generations, even after the תקופת הגאונים
    - **מהרש״ל (גיטין)**
        - Writes אין דרך להיות לאדם שני שמות כאחד
    - **נחלת שבעה**
        - Mentions hearing that in some places two names are given at the ברית (such as יוסף ויצחק), implying it was not done in his own community
    - Rabbonim historically objected to giving two names (שלא כהוגן ומיחו בהם), but because there was no איסור גמור, the protests did not stop the practice
    - Conclusion: בזמן הזה there is no halachic prohibition against giving two names

## Naming After Two Different People

- Giving two names is often used as a שלום בית compromise between parents
- **ספר ברית אבות**
    - Cites גדולים who rule not to name a child after two different people
    - Particularly applies to two individuals who did not get along in life (such as מחותנים), based on kabbalistic considerations
- Issue of whether combining two names creates a third, distinct name:
    - Combining two names might be considered a new independent name rather than naming after either person
    - If it is not their name, it may not extend the person's מזל or create a זכות for the נשמה
        - Rav Schechter quoted חזון איש: naming after a family member is a זכות for the relative, whereas naming after a צדיק is a זכות for the child
- **Rav Leizer Silver (משולחן גבוה, סוף פרשת מטות)**
    - Ruled for the Telz ראשי ישיבה that a father named Yitzchak Isaac could name his son Avraham Yitzchak (applied when Rav Yitzchok Isaac Ausband named his son Rav Avraham Yitzchok Ausband)
    - **Q:** how can a father give his son a name that contains his own name (Yitzchak)?
    - **A:** Avraham Yitzchak and Yitzchak Isaac are completely distinct names with nothing to do with each other
    - Proof from חומש:
        - יאיר conquered villages and named them חוות יאיר; the תורה does not say "בשמו" because he added the word חוות
        - נבח conquered קנת and named it נבח בשמו; the תורה says "בשמו" because the name was kept exactly identical without additions
        - Adding or altering any component creates an entirely new name rather than calling someone by the original name
- **דעת זקנים מבעלי התוספות**
    - Explains that יוסף named his son אפרים after both אברהם ("אנכי עפר ואפר") and יצחק ("אפר על גבי המזבח" at the עקידה) — double אפר forms אפרים
    - Demonstrates an early precedent for naming after two different people by combining their names into one (comparable to the מהרש״ל's account of מאיר and יאיר becoming שניאור)

## שינוי השם for Illness

- Another circumstance where a person acquires an additional name is during serious illness
- **גמ׳ (ראש השנה דף ט״ז, תענית דף ט״ז, בבא קמא דף קכ״ה)**
    - שינוי השם is one of the things that tears an evil decree (קורע גזירה רעה) by altering the person's מזל
- **בית יוסף (יורה דעה סימן של״ח)**
    - Records widespread practice to change or add a name during critical illness to remove the decree
- Performed in consultation with גדולים or a צדיק:
    - A מדרש states that parents possess רוח הקודש when naming an infant at birth
    - Later in life, only a great צדיק (or שוטים, per גמ׳) possesses the requisite insight to determine an appropriate name
- Halachic status of the added name:
    - If the patient recovers, the added name remains permanent
        - Ex: Rav Yisroel Belsky was severely ill ~7 years before his passing and had the name חיים added, remaining Rav Chaim Yisroel Belsky for the rest of his life
    - If the patient dies from that illness, the added name is dropped and does not stick

## Using Secular and Non-Jewish Names

- Raised via question regarding יוסף being called צפנת פענח
- **אגרות משה**
    - Discusses the permissibility of using secular or non-Jewish names, noting that many אמוראים carried secular names
    - **Q:** how is this permitted if בני ישראל were redeemed from מצרים in the merit of שלא שינו את שמם?
    - **A:** prior to מתן תורה, external distinctions (names, dress, language) were the only markers identifying Jews; once the תורה was given, תרי״ג מצוות define and distinguish the Jewish people, so שלא שינו את שמם is no longer the defining factor
- Non-Jewish names with religious connotations:
    - Any name implying an allusion to objects of worship in other religions is highly objectionable
    - Secular names without religious connotations (e.g. historical precedent of Rabbeinu Peter among בעלי התוספות, or common names like John and Paul) are not necessarily prohibited, as Rav Moshe demonstrates extensive precedent for secular names
