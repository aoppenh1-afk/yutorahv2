# קריאת שני שמות

## הקדמה

- Giving a child two names is practically absent in תנ״ך, גמ׳, and ראשונים, where almost everyone has a single name
- The shiur is not addressing the historical reasons why the מנהג arose
    - Not whether it began after a mass casualty event where multiple relatives needed to be remembered w/ fewer children
    - Not whether it arose in a specific community as a שלום בית compromise between sets of parents
    - Not whether compound names evolved from paired Hebrew-Yiddish names (such as יששכר בער or צבי הירש) expanding into triple or quadruple names
- The shiur addresses three core questions
    - Did two names ever exist historically in earlier generations, and how do we interpret apparent double names in the גמ׳?
    - Is there any halachic issue w/ giving two names under normal circumstances (e.g. to avoid choosing between relatives or to dilute a name a child might dislike)?
    - What is the הלכה regarding naming after two different people?

## ראיות מש״ס וראשונים

- **תנ״ך**
    - Figures such as משה רבינו and יתרו had multiple names, but they are never called by more than one name simultaneously
- **נודע ביהודה (מהדורא תנינא, או״ח סימן קי״ג)**
    - States that in all of ש״ס, in both בבלי and ירושלמי, not a single תנא or אמורא has two names
    - **Q:** what about apparent double names in ש״ס?
    - **A1:** the term בריבי (e.g. רב אושעיא בריבי, רבי אלעזר הקפר בריבי, חזקיה בריבי)
        - When בריבי appears alone, it is a proper שם חכם
        - When attached to another name, רש״י consistently explains it is a title denoting the גדול הדור
        - **רש״י:** notes בריבי is a שם חכם whenever it stands alone because usually it functions as a title
            - Exception in חולין דף י״א
            - In חולין דף נ״ז and סוטה דף כ״ט, בריבי appears alone and רש״י explains it refers to other well-known אמוראים (חזקיה and רבי יוסי), which is evident from context
    - **A2:** names prefixed w/ אבא (e.g. אבא שאול, אבא יוסי)
        - אבא is an honorific title of authority and כבוד, similar to addressing a father
        - An עבד never carries the title אבא, except the עבדים של רבן גמליאל who held exceptional חשיבות
        - אימא שלום was named שלום; אימא was a title honoring her חשיבות
            - שלום was used historically as a woman's name, functioning as a unisex name similar to how יונה is used today
    - **A3:** the name עי מרי (גיטין דף ל״ה)
        - **רש״י:** comments that it is a שם חניכה (a surname or nickname)
        - **תורת חסד:** argues people might have used two names for a שם חניכה but not for an actual name, or that עי is a title similar to אבא
- **ראשונים**
    - Extremely rare, but not entirely unprecedented
    - **יעקב ישראל:** a בעל התוספות mentioned in תוס׳ כתובות דף צ״ח, חולין דף קי״ב, and ספר הישר לרבינו תם; both names belonged to the same individual
    - **מהרש״ל (ים של שלמה גיטין פרק ד׳)**
        - Relates a case of parents arguing over naming after the father's father (מאיר) vs the mother's father (יאיר), resolving it by combining both into the single name שניאור (שני אור)
        - Proof that giving two separate names (יאיר מאיר) was unheard of, requiring a single name that encapsulated both
            - Alternatively, naming him מאיר יאיר would not have solved the dispute over which grandfather took precedence
        - The name שניאור itself predated this incident
            - Mentioned by the רמב״ן
            - **חיד״א (מערכת גדולים):** notes that רבינו יונה had a rebbe named שניאור
            - The parents simply chose an existing name because it fit both grandfathers

## שינוי השם לחולה

- Adding a second name is established in cases of severe illness
    - **גמ׳ ראש השנה דף ט״ז, תענית דף ט״ז, ב״ק דף קכ״ה:** שינוי השם is one of the ways to tear up a גזירה רעה by changing a person's מזל
    - **בית יוסף (יו״ד סימן של״ח):** cites the established practice of adding a name when someone is desperately ill
    - The added name remains permanent only if the patient recovers; if the patient passes away from that illness, the added name is dropped
        - Ex: Rav Yisroel Belsky had חיים added during a critical illness seven years before his death, so he became Rav Chaim Yisroel Belsky
    - An added name requires consultation w/ a גדול
        - Parents possess a level of רוח הקודש when initially naming a baby
        - Later in life, only a צדיק can be trusted to direct the name change

## האם יש איסור בשני שמות?

- **נודע ביהודה and חתם סופר**
    - Do not formally prohibit two names, but their tone reflects clear disapproval
- **חזון איש (תשובות והנהגות חלק א׳, סימן תר״ו)**
    - Strongly disliked the practice of giving two names, even though his own name was אברהם ישעיה
    - Reason stems from the severe complexities it creates in הלכות גיטין
        - In גיטין, determining which name is primary (דמיקרי) is critical because errors threaten the יחוס of כלל ישראל
        - In contrast to כתובות, where stakes are lower, גיטין require absolute precision regarding every known name
- **אגרות משה (או״ח חלק ה׳, סימן י׳, אות ג׳)**
    - Views the חזון איש as somewhat of a דעת יחיד on prohibiting it
    - Confirms that calling someone by two names together did not exist in earlier generations, even after the period of the גאונים
        - **מהרש״ל (גיטין):** writes אין דרך להיות לאדם שני שמות כאחד
        - **נחלת שבעה:** records hearing that in some places two names were given at a ברית (such as יוסף ויצחק), implying it was foreign to his own locale
    - Historically the practice was שלא כהוגן and rabbonim protested, but because it was not an איסור גמור, the protest was not universally accepted
    - **מסקנא:** there is no איסור to give two names בזמן הזה

## קריאה על שם שני אנשים

- **ספר ברית אבות**
    - Quotes גדולים who caution against naming a child after two different people
    - Particularly applies when the two individuals did not get along during their lifetimes (such as opposing מחותנים), based primarily on קבלה
- Loss of the original identity and מזל
    - A compound name may be halachically viewed as a brand-new third name rather than a continuation of either individual
    - If viewed as a new name, the purpose of extending the person's מזל or bringing a זכות to the נשמה is lost
    - **חזון איש (acc to Rav Schechter):** naming after a family member brings a זכות to the relative, whereas naming after a צדיק brings a זכות to the child; a blended name risks providing a זכות to neither
- **Rav Leizer Silver (ספר משולחן גבוה, פרשת מטות)**
    - Issued a פסק to the Telz ראשי ישיבה permitting Rav Yitzchok Isaac Ausband to name his son Rav Avraham Yitzchok Ausband
    - **יסוד:** Yitzchak Isaac and Avraham Yitzchak are two completely unrelated, distinct names
    - **Proof from חומש (פרשת מטות):**
        - When יאיר captured villages, he named them חוות יאיר, and the פסוק does not write בשמו because the addition of the prefix חוות altered the name
        - When נבח captured קנת, he named it נבח, and the פסוק explicitly states ויקרא לה נבח בשמו because he kept the name identical w/o additions
        - Any modification or addition creates an independent name
- **דעת זקנים מבעלי התוספות**
    - יוסף named אפרים after both אברהם (אנכי עפר ואפר) and יצחק (who was like אפר on the מזבח)
    - Double אפר produced אפרים, demonstrating that one can name after two people through an encapsulating name rather than exact names

## שמות שאינם יהודיים

- **אגרות משה**
    - Explains why using secular or non-Jewish names is permissible, noting that numerous אמוראים in the גמ׳ carried names of non-Jewish origin
    - **Q:** does this not contradict the midrash that ישראל were redeemed from מצרים because שלא שינו את שמם?
    - **A:** שלא שינו את שמם was vital in מצרים because prior to מתן תורה, external identifiers (clothing, language, and names) were the only factors distinguishing Jews from the surrounding nations
        - After מתן תורה, the observance of תרי״ג מצוות defines and preserves Jewish identity, so adopting secular names is not intrinsically forbidden
- Non-Jewish names w/ religious connotations
    - Any secular name that alludes to objects of religious worship in other religions is strictly objectionable
    - Conventional secular or historical names (such as John, Paul, or historical figures like רבינו פטר) are commonly found among Jews and are difficult to categorize as אסור
